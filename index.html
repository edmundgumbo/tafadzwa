          Into the Breach, episode 1: Breaking the firewall

          OPSWAT docuseries hosted by Kari Byron, filmed at Black Hat, RSA and DEF CON. Made Season 2.

          Key takeaways
          • A firewall is a checkpoint that allows or blocks traffic using rules people write.
          • Rules run in order, so one badly placed rule can open a gap.
          • Allow-everything rules and thousands of unmanaged rules are common real-world failures.
          • Attackers fingerprint the firewall, look up known flaws and even study patches to build exploits.
          • Two firewalls help most when they are different brands, at a cost in complexity and speed.
          • Data diodes enforce one-way flow in hardware, for critical systems like power and water.
          Our call

          Good, practical episode, but it is made by OPSWAT, which sells data diodes. Treat "unhackable" as the sponsor's claim: a diode closes the return path, but people, suppliers and bad data flowing the allowed way are still risks. The flamethrower and robot dog are TV props.

              Building a cybersecurity career in the age of AI

              Kat in Cyber livestream with guest Faan Rossouw, 15 Oct 2026. Added modules 8 and 9.

              Security+ : the real exam, made simple

              This season maps to CompTIA Security+, the beginner certificate employers ask for most. Learn it here, then sit the real exam to get certified. It covers five domains. All five are live, plus free labs and a timed mock exam at the bottom.

              Domain 1: General security concepts

              Exam view: Domain 1 facts to memorise The five domains (SY0-801, official weights)
              1. General Security Concepts, 16% (this one)
              2. Threats, Vulnerabilities, and Attacks, 24%
              3. Security Architecture, 19%
              4. Security Operations, 27%
              5. Security Program Management and Oversight, 14%
              Domain 1 key terms
              • CIA triad: Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability.
              • AAA: Authentication (who you are), Authorization (what you may do), Accounting (what you did).
              • Auth factors: something you know, something you have, something you are. Two different types = MFA.
              • Least privilege: give the smallest access needed.
              • Zero trust: verify every request, never trust by location.
              • Defense in depth: many layers, physical + technical + administrative.
              • Encryption: reversible with a key. In transit (HTTPS/TLS) vs at rest (disk/db).
              • Hashing: one-way fingerprint. Store passwords hashed, never encrypted or plain.

              The real exam is SY0-701 now; CompTIA confirms SY0-801 launches 17 Nov 2026 and 701 retires about mid 2027. As a 2027 learner, aim for SY0-801. Always check CompTIA's site for the current date before booking.

                Domain 2: Threats, vulnerabilities and attacks

                About 24% of the exam. Who attacks, how they get in, the weak spots they use, the signs they leave, and the new AI risks.

                Exam view: Domain 2 facts to memorise The five official topics
                1. Common threat actors and their motivations
                2. Threat vectors and attack surfaces
                3. Indicators of malicious activity
                4. Common vulnerabilities and their risks
                5. Security risks of artificial intelligence (new)
                Threat actors
                • Nation-state / APT: most resources, patient, spying or disruption.
                • Organised crime: money. Hacktivist: a cause. Unskilled attacker: fame or fun.
                • Insider: already has access, often revenge or money. Shadow IT / accidental: no intent, still harmful.
                • Compare by: internal vs external, sophistication, resources and funding.
                Vectors and social engineering
                • Vectors: email, SMS, IM, attachments, QR codes, USB, open Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, NFC, supply chain, watering hole.
                • Phishing (email), spear (one person), whaling (a boss), smishing (SMS), vishing (voice).
                • Pretexting (fake story), BEC (payment or bank change by email), typosquatting (look-alike address), tailgating, shoulder surfing.
                Vulnerabilities
                • Unpatched or end-of-life systems, misconfiguration, default passwords, open ports, zero-days.
                • Hardcoded secrets, race conditions, poor error handling, stale credentials, rogue devices, weak crypto.
                • CVE = the flaw's ID. CVSS = score 0 to 10 (9.0+ Critical, 7.0 to 8.9 High). NVD = the public database.
                Attacks
                • Malware: ransomware (locks), trojan (disguised), worm (spreads alone), virus (needs a click), spyware/keylogger (watches), rootkit (hides), logic bomb (waits).
                • Passwords: brute force (one account, every guess), spraying (one password, many accounts), credential stuffing (leaked pairs reused).
                • Apps: SQL injection, XSS, buffer overflow. Network: DDoS, on-path, DNS poisoning, replay.
                Indicators and AI
                • Indicators: impossible travel, account lockouts, concurrent sessions, resource spikes, missing or odd logs, blocked content, data published online.
                • AI risks: prompt injection, data poisoning, model theft and inversion, excessive agency, insecure output handling, deepfakes, AI phishing.

                Checked against CompTIA's SY0-801 page on 1 Nov 2026. Download the full official objectives PDF from CompTIA before you book.

                  Domain 3: Security architecture

                  About 19% of the exam. Where systems live, walls inside the network, safe remote access, protecting data, and staying up when things break.

                  Exam view: Domain 3 facts to memorise The four official topics
                  1. Security of cloud, hybrid, on-premises and OT architectures
                  2. Securing enterprise infrastructure: access control, segmentation, connectivity
                  3. Protecting data through its lifecycle
                  4. Resilience and recovery: backups, redundancy, recovery testing
                  Architecture
                  • IaaS (raw servers), PaaS (platform for your code), SaaS (finished app). Shared responsibility: provider secures the cloud, you secure what is in it.
                  • Containers, serverless, microservices, IaC (servers built from files). OT/IoT: hard to patch, isolate them.
                  Infrastructure
                  • Segmentation, screened subnet (DMZ), VLANs, air gap, jump server.
                  • Firewall, WAF, IDS (detects), IPS (blocks), proxy, load balancer, NAC, 802.1X with RADIUS.
                  • Fail-open lets traffic through when broken, fail-closed blocks it. VPN (IPsec, TLS), full vs split tunnel, SASE, SD-WAN.
                  Data
                  • Labels: public, private, sensitive, confidential, restricted, critical. States: at rest, in transit, in use.
                  • Encryption, hashing, masking (****1234), tokenization (swap for a token), obfuscation, DLP, data sovereignty, geolocation.
                  Resilience
                  • High availability, redundancy, clustering, load balancing, UPS and generator, multi-cloud.
                  • Sites: hot (ready now), warm (partly), cold (empty space).
                  • Backups: full, incremental (since last backup), differential (since last full), snapshots, replication, 3-2-1. Test restores. Tabletop, failover and simulation tests.

                  Story view: play through Domain 3

                    Domain 4: Security operations

                    The biggest domain, about 27% of the exam. The day job of a security analyst.

                    Exam view: Domain 4 facts to memorise The eight official topics
                    1. Security techniques for endpoints, networks, applications and data
                    2. Asset management across the lifecycle
                    3. Finding, prioritising and fixing vulnerabilities
                    4. Security monitoring and alerting
                    5. Identity and access management, federation and privileged access
                    6. Automation and orchestration, including AI-assisted workflows
                    7. Incident response, from preparation to lessons learned
                    8. Data sources for investigations
                    Hardening and assets
                    • Secure baselines, remove extras, patch, disk encryption, host firewall, EDR/XDR, MDM, WPA3 + RADIUS.
                    • Email: SPF (allowed senders), DKIM (signature), DMARC (policy + reports). DNS filtering.
                    • Assets: inventory, owner, classification. Retire with sanitization, degaussing or destruction, plus a certificate of destruction.
                    Vulnerabilities and monitoring
                    • Credentialed vs non-credentialed scans, pen tests, bug bounty, OSINT and threat feeds.
                    • False positive (alert, no flaw), false negative (flaw, no alert). Patch, compensating control, exception, rescan, report.
                    • SIEM, log aggregation, agents, alert tuning, baselines, SNMP, NetFlow, archiving.
                    Identity
                    • Provisioning and deprovisioning, SSO, federation, SAML, OAuth (access without your password), OpenID Connect, passkeys, MFA.
                    • RBAC (role), ABAC (attributes), rule-based, MAC (labels), DAC (owner decides). PAM: vaulted, just-in-time admin access.
                    Automation and incidents
                    • SOAR playbooks, guardrails, ticketing. Risks: complexity, single point of failure, technical debt.
                    • IR order: Preparation, Detection, Analysis, Containment, Eradication, Recovery, Lessons learned.
                    • Tabletop, simulation, threat hunting, root cause analysis. Forensics: legal hold, chain of custody, order of volatility (memory before disk).
                    • Data sources: firewall, application, endpoint, OS security, IDS/IPS logs, packet captures, metadata, scans, dashboards.

                    Story view: play through Domain 4

                      Domain 5: Security program management and oversight

                      About 14% of the exam. Rules, risk maths, suppliers, the law, audits and training. Easy marks once it clicks.

                      Exam view: Domain 5 facts to memorise The six official topics
                      1. Security governance: policies, standards and procedures
                      2. Security risk management
                      3. Third-party risk management
                      4. Compliance and privacy
                      5. Audits and assessments
                      6. Security awareness
                      Governance
                      • Policy (what and why), standard (exact rule), procedure (how), guideline (advice). AUP, change management, IR, BC, DR policies.
                      • Data owner (accountable), custodian/steward (day to day), controller (decides why), processor (handles for the controller).
                      Risk
                      • SLE = AV × EF. ALE = SLE × ARO. Qualitative vs quantitative. Risk register, owner, appetite, tolerance, KRIs.
                      • Responses: mitigate, transfer, accept, avoid. BIA: RTO (max downtime), RPO (max data loss in time), MTTR, MTBF.
                      Third parties, compliance, audits
                      • Due diligence, questionnaires, right to audit, monitoring. SLA, MSA, SOW, NDA, MOU/MOA, BPA, rules of engagement.
                      • Compliance reporting, fines, sanctions, loss of licence. Privacy: data subject rights, right to be forgotten, retention, data inventory.
                      • Internal, external, regulatory audits, self-assessment, attestation. Pen tests: known, partially known, unknown environment.
                      • Awareness: phishing campaigns, training, reporting, measuring results.

                        Mock exam

                        30 questions in 30 minutes, weighted like the real exam, including hands-on style matching and ordering tasks. No feedback until the end, just like test day. Anything you get wrong goes into your daily review.
